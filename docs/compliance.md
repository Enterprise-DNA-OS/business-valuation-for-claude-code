# Evidence checks, not professional certification

Sources checked 16 November 2026. These checks find missing records. They do not establish compliance or replace a qualified practitioner's judgement.

[APES 225 Valuation Services, October 2024](https://apesb.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/APES_225_Oct_2024.pdf) applies from January 2025. Sections 3, 4, 5 and 6 address independence, engagement terms, reporting and documentation. The workbench flags missing independence notes, scope fields and evidence. Purpose, intended users, basis and premise sit beside every draft. It does not test every report disclosure, engagement classification, competence requirement or professional obligation. Applicability belongs to the practitioner.

[New Zealand Privacy Principle 9](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/9/) limits keeping personal information beyond its lawful purpose. The operator sets a retention review date; an overdue date triggers review, never automatic deletion. Legal holds and other retention duties must be considered before disposal.

Independent review, stale-evidence dates and the two-year comparable flag are practice policies. They are not prescribed legal periods. Reviews name the engagement revision. Changes to financials, forecasts, adjustments, scenarios, comparables, evidence or scope invalidate the current review. Reviewer and preparer must differ. Named actors are audit labels, not authenticated identities. Acceptance records a review decision, not authority to distribute an opinion.

The database is for trusted operators. Public database roles have no access, and row security is enabled. Provision restricted roles, private storage, tested backups and user authentication before shared deployment. Rendered documents, exports and drafts contain confidential information; keep them private. No sends, live feeds or data removal are automated.
